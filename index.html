Research interests

Central interest

Neuro-symbolic AI

How can neural models learn from data while reasoning with explicit knowledge and constraints? I'm interested in combining language models with knowledge graphs, ontologies, and symbolic verification to make multi-step decisions more traceable.

My focus is on graph-constrained reasoning, smaller language models, and agents that can check their next step against domain knowledge.

Deep reinforcement learning

Learning useful decision policies in structured environments, with rewards that reflect valid actions and task completion.

Post-training & small models

Adapting language models through LoRA and reinforcement-learning methods such as GRPO, with an emphasis on efficient, dependable reasoning.

Knowledge-grounded agents

Connecting graph-based retrieval, planning, and tool use so an agent's decisions can be inspected against their supporting evidence.

Fairness & uncertainty

Methods for identifying bias and fairness issues, improving interpretability, and understanding predictive uncertainty, particularly in healthcare AI.

Underlying these interests: applied mathematics, optimization, and graph algorithms.

Ideas & notes

Open questions at the intersection of language models, structured knowledge, and decision-making.

Open question · Neuro-symbolic AI

Can a graph make a reasoning path easier to verify?

I'm interested in using explicit relationships and constraints to guide each step of a language model's reasoning. Can we check whether a chosen path follows the graph, uses the right evidence, and reaches a supported conclusion?

A valid graph path is a starting point; establishing that it captures the right causal relationships is a further question. This distinction motivates my interest in symbolic verification and traceable decision-making.

Related reading: Knowledge Graphs ↗

Open question · Post-training

Small models for structured decisions

What changes when a model chooses among a few valid next actions instead of generating an unconstrained plan? I'm interested in combining small language models, graph-derived candidate actions, and rule-based rewards.

The question is how much reasoning can be learned within that structure, and how to evaluate accuracy, latency, and failure cases together.
